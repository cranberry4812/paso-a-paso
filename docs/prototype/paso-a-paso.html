Paso a Paso is a free online program for Spanish-speaking children. In 12 weeks, they learn English words, build early technology skills, and discover what architects, doctors, musicians and storytellers do.

For parents and caregivers

Why Paso a Paso is good for your child

Short, playful sessions in English, with Spanish support so the whole family can take part.

English early, the easy way

Young children pick up new sounds and words quickly. Songs, games and one power phrase per mission make English feel like play.

Parents can follow along

Every word is shown in English and Spanish, so you can practice at home even if you're still learning English yourself.

The first steps of coding

Children practice putting steps in order, spotting patterns and repeating loops. These are the ideas behind coding, learned with blocks, drums and story cards.

Little screen time

Hands-on activities come first. Screen activities last 5 minutes at most per session, always with an adult nearby.

Designed by professionals

Architects, doctors, music producers and storytellers help design each mission, so children see real jobs they could have one day.

Made for little attention spans

Sessions are 25 minutes, twice a week, and follow the same five steps every time, so children always know what comes next.

Confidence and pride

Each mission ends with a small showcase for the family. At the end of the program, every child gets a certificate.

Free, and from home

No travel and no special equipment. Most materials are things you already have at home.

Safe by design

No accounts for children, very little personal information, and a co-facilitator in every session. Read how we keep children safe.

What your family needs

  • A phone, tablet or computer with a camera and internet connection
  • An adult nearby during sessions (required for ages 4 and 5)
  • Blocks or small boxes, paper and crayons
  • A stuffed animal (for the doctor mission)
  • A pot, box or plastic container to use as a drum

Weak internet? Each session has an offline version of the activity, so children can still take part if the connection drops.

Volunteer partners

Help design a mission

We're looking for volunteer professionals to co-design the activities for one mission. You don't need to teach or appear on camera, though you're welcome to join a session live.

How co-design works

  1. You receive a one-page design brief
  2. You draft the activities and word list for your field
  3. The program lead adapts them to be bilingual and age-appropriate
  4. You review the final version
  5. We run it and send you the families' feedback

Roles we need

Mission 1

Architect

About 6–8 hours per term

  • Building activities and step cards
  • 8–10 photos of homes and buildings with parts labeled
  • Review of the home vocabulary
  • Written feedback on children's home designs
Mission 2

Doctor

About 5–6 hours per term

  • Healthy-habits content for ages 4 to 7
  • Check-up play script for the stuffed-animal game
  • Accuracy review of body and health words
  • Health education only. No advice to individual families.
Mission 3

Music producer

About 6–8 hours per term

  • Rhythm and pattern games
  • A 4-beat song template children can play along with
  • Choice of sounds for the digital activities
Mission 4

Storyteller

About 4–6 hours per term

  • 6–8 story cards in English and Spanish
  • Characters and simple plots for ages 4 to 7
  • Help judging the family festival

Safety and privacy

How we keep children safe

In every session

  • A co-facilitator is always present
  • No private one-on-one contact between adults and children
  • Waiting room and passcode on every call
  • An adult nearby for children ages 4 and 5
  • Partners never contact children directly

What we collect

  • Child's first name or nickname
  • Age group (4–5 or 6–7)
  • A signed consent form, kept by the partner organization

What we never collect

  • Last names, birthdates or addresses
  • School names
  • Children's accounts on any tool
  • Photos or recordings without written consent

Everyone who works directly with children completes a background check. A written safeguarding policy, reviewed by a child development professional, will be shared with families before the first session.
